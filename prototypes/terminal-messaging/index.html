Direction A

Designed Surface

Calm and premium. The terminal becomes a first-class inset surface — soft rounded frame, top-lit ambient glow, faint grain — with the composer floating over it.

  • Feels: considered, atmospheric
  • Hero: the surface itself
  • Risk: glow/grain can feel “much” for power users (opt-outable)
Direction B

Warm Front Door

Friendliest. A welcome mat with a critter, plain-language greeting and one-tap starters; the message input is the hero, centred when empty and docked when running, with a hint band.

  • Feels: welcoming, human
  • Hero: the empty state + first 10 seconds
  • Risk: can read “consumer” — dial warmth to taste
Direction C

Clean & Minimal

Tidiest. No glow, no grain — restraint. Readable output with soft-syntax (paths, URLs, diffs, errors), gutter status glyphs and breathing room; a slim, monochrome single-line composer.

  • Feels: precise, pro-tool
  • Hero: legible output + spacing
  • Risk: less “friendly” on its own — leans on copy
Notes for review. These are static HTML mocks — no real terminal, PTY or Claude — for look-and-feel only, and they change nothing in the app. The working indicator now uses the real animated critters from Resources/Critters (random per view). Defaults are now Ink / Linen palette + DM Mono · 14 / 2.5 / −0.03em / 400. All three keep the principle from the research doc: never touch what SwiftTerm draws — the styling layers around / behind the grid and restyles IDEalize’s own re-render. Mix-and-match is expected (e.g. A’s surface + C’s readable output).
